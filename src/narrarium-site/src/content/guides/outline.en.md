# The Synopsis

The Synopsis is the plan of the book, chapter by chapter: the idea of the book (its premise, the pitch) and, for every chapter, a hypothetical title, what happens in it and, when it is not the book's, the ghostwriter that writes it. Open it from the book's menu (**Synopsis**). It lives in the files of the book (`outline/book.md` and one `outline/chapters/<NNN>.md` per chapter), so it travels with the book like everything else.

## The page

At the top, **The book** is one card: a working title (optional: the book's own title stays in its settings), the **Idea, promise or pitch** (what the book is about, who it is about and what is at stake, in a sentence or two; the autopilot builds the concept and the plan from it), the genre, the audience, the **Words per scene** and the **Chapter-by-chapter plot** (optional). **Save all** (or Ctrl+S) saves the whole card at once: the idea and the title in the book, the brief fields in `outline/_brief.md`. The idea is the description of the book in `outline/book.md`; there is no other. Below, one card per chapter with its **Hypothetical title**, **What happens** (plain text: the events, the turning points, who is there; not prose) and its **Ghostwriter**, where **The book's ghostwriter (default)** is the first choice.

Every card says in words, with an icon, what became of it: **Written by the autopilot, untouched**, **Yours**, **Chapter already written** (a link to the chapter), **Frozen chapter**, **Chapter approved**, or **Changed after the plan: the autopilot will plan it again**. A ghostwriter that does not exist, and a file the Synopsis cannot read, are said on the card.

The buttons of a card move it up or down (also with Alt+↑ and Alt+↓ inside the card), insert a chapter before or after it, or delete it. Deleting asks first: a chapter already written keeps its text in the book, and the autopilot does not delete it; **Renumber the following chapters** closes the gap. **Add a chapter at the end** is at the top of the page. After every action **Undo** takes it back exactly.

## Saving

What you type is saved when you choose **Save** on the card, or **Save all** (also Ctrl+S) for everything: until then the card says **Unsaved changes**, and leaving the page asks first. Nothing is saved while you type, on purpose: the autopilot plans a chapter again as soon as its entry changes. Every save is a commit of the book, published like the others. If the Synopsis changed elsewhere meanwhile (the autopilot, the Copilot, another tab), your text is kept and the page says so; **Reload the synopsis** shows the new version and discards what you had not saved.

## Long books

The page shows 50 chapters at a time, with **Previous** and **Next**. **Filter chapters…** finds chapters by number, title or what happens, and **Go to chapter** jumps to one. A Synopsis of 5000 chapters opens as fast as one of ten.

## An empty Synopsis

**Add the first chapter**, **Let the autopilot write it** (the autopilot's **Configuration** opens), or, when the brief has a chapter-by-chapter plot, **Convert the brief's plot**: the lines «Chapter 1: …» become chapters, the rest becomes the idea of the book, and you see everything before anything is written.

## What the autopilot does with it

The autopilot follows the Synopsis as binding: the same chapters, in the same order, with your titles and your events. When it plans, it fills the chapters that have no entry yet, as its own (**Written by the autopilot, untouched**), and never touches yours; as soon as you change an entry it wrote, the entry is yours. When you change the entry of a chapter it has not written yet, it plans that chapter again (**Replanning chapter N** in its history); when you delete one, the chapter leaves its plan and the following chapters keep their numbers (a chapter already written stays in the book and in its plan: the autopilot only tells you, once, that its entry is gone). When you change the entry of a chapter it has already written and closed, it does what its **Configuration** says for a changed frozen chapter: by default it asks you what to do: **Rewrite the chapter to follow the Synopsis**, **Keep the text, update only the Synopsis** or **Mark as reviewed** (see *Frozen chapters* in the autopilot's help). A chapter it has in its hands, being written or revised, is told once and decided when it is closed. The card says so before you save, and the page says it again once saved. A chapter takes the ghostwriter of its entry; one that does not exist is said, and the book's writes it. **Fill the Synopsis with the plan**, in the autopilot's **Configuration** (under **The book**), turns the filling off. The chapter-by-chapter plot of the brief still works, and where the two differ the Synopsis wins.

## What changed

**What changed**, at the top of the page, lists the chapters whose entry the autopilot has not followed yet (it compares each entry with the one its plan follows), and what else changed since you last chose **Mark as reviewed** (before the first time, since the autopilot last worked). For each chapter it says what happened (new, changed, moved from another chapter, removed), who did it (you, Brainstorming, the autopilot, an assistant through MCP), when, and what the autopilot will do about it, in the words the page uses after a save; the card of each chapter of the list says it too, with a label. The history comes from `outline/changes.yml`, the journal of the Synopsis: every series of edits saved by the page, the Copilot, Brainstorming, the autopilot or an assistant through MCP adds a few lines to it, in the same commit. It keeps the last 180 days (at most 200 series), and what two devices wrote apart is merged. A change made outside the app is listed too, without who and when; the autopilot never decides from the journal, so deleting it loses only the history. **Mark as reviewed** folds the list: what the autopilot has not followed yet stays counted and labelled. For a book whose autopilot has no approved plan the list is only the history: the autopilot follows the Synopsis as it is when it plans the book.

## Apply to the book

When the entry of a chapter that is already written and closed changed, **What changed** offers **Apply to the book…**. The panel lists every such chapter with what changed, whether it is approved and who froze it, and what the autopilot would do without you (as its **Configuration** says for a changed frozen chapter); for each you choose **Rewrite it to follow the Synopsis**, **Keep the text, update only the Synopsis** or **Decide later**. A chapter the autopilot wrote starts as rewritten; one you wrote before the autopilot, one you froze by hand and one you left for later start as **Decide later**: the autopilot never rewrites a chapter you wrote by its own decision, only when you choose it here. The other changes are told (a chapter not written yet is planned again, a new one at the end is planned after the plan). You can add words for the director, for all the chapters and for each one, and the panel says what it will cost: the chapters to rewrite at most (as if written anew), then the rest of the plan.

Nothing changes until you confirm the chapters that will be rewritten. Then the autopilot starts (or, if it is running, takes them at its next step), rewrites them one at a time in the order of the book, each reviewed by the director and frozen again, and goes on with its plan. **What changed**, the band of the autopilot and its mini-player say which chapter is being rewritten and which wait; **Cancel the chapters that wait** leaves them for later (the one being rewritten is finished). A chapter left for later is neither asked about nor rewritten until you decide, here or on its page. It works the same for a book kept on this device and for one that runs on your personal server, which must be recent enough: an older server is told to update, and nothing is sent to it. If the page ends during the work, the autopilot resumes it when the app opens again.

## The assistant in the text areas

The menu of a text area of the Synopsis (right click, or the round button on a touch screen) offers **Improve the entry**, **Summarize the entry**, **Develop this chapter** (events and key points consistent with the description, the nearby chapters and the canon) and **Check against the canon and the nearby chapters** (what does not hold). Each shows its answer first: **Apply** puts it in the card as an unsaved change, **Cancel** leaves the card as it was. Your custom actions for the book appear there too.

## Talking to the Copilot about it

On the Synopsis page and on the autopilot's pages, «chapter 3» means entry 3 of the Synopsis; on every other page of the book it means the written chapter 3. «Synopsis» (or «outline», «sinossi», «scaletta») always means the Synopsis, wherever you are, and «the written chapter» or «the chapter's text» always means the chapter. When it is not clear, the Copilot asks which one. Its changes to the Synopsis are a proposal: nothing is written until you choose **Apply all file updates**, they are applied together and saved like the page's saves (**Undo assistant changes** takes them back), and every entry it writes is yours.

## Brainstorming

**Brainstorming** is the Copilot powered up for the big design of the book: the premise, the arcs, the pacing, the stakes, the structure. Open it with **Brainstorming** at the top of the Synopsis page, or with its orange button beside the Copilot's at the bottom right, which is there while the page is open. It works only on the Synopsis of this book: the idea of the book and every chapter entry, the description of the book and its brief, the summaries of the chapters already written and whether they are approved, frozen or imported, and the names of the canon (never the secrets). It writes no prose, never touches the text of a chapter and has none of the Copilot's other tools: it reads an entry, the summary of a chapter or an entry of the canon when it needs them, runs the coherence check of the whole Synopsis, and proposes changes.

An empty chat offers ideas to begin with, to **Start the book** (from a premise to a synopsis, the heart of the story, three ways to tell it, the arcs of the cast) and to **Change the book** (what if…, restructure, add a subplot, reorder the chapters, tighten the middle, check the Synopsis): each one fills in your request, which you change and send.

A change of the Synopsis is always a proposal: a series of edits (new chapters, moves, changes, deletions) made as the Synopsis page makes them, with the difference of every file and **What the autopilot will do** about each chapter it touches, in the words of the page: an approved chapter is asked about, rewritten or only reported as its **Configuration** says, and one not written yet is planned again. **Apply to the Synopsis** asks once more; the changes are then saved together, as yours, and **Undo** takes them back. When you ask, Brainstorming also saves the decisions of a brainstorm in the notes of the book, `outline/brainstorm.md`, a dated section each time: they are not entries, and the autopilot does not follow them.

Brainstorming keeps its own chats for each book: opening it again brings back the latest, and its **History** lists only the chats of the book. **Back to Copilot** in its header, the Copilot's own button, or leaving the page bring the Copilot back with its chat as it was. It works for a book kept on this device and without GitHub, and it changes the Synopsis only while «Edit the Synopsis» is on in **Settings → Tools for Copilot**. It has its own row in **Settings → AI Router**, **Brainstorming**, under the Copilot's group: without a route of its own it follows the Copilot's route (its models and its levels of thinking), and without that either it goes where the Copilot goes, so it needs no setting; give it a route of its own to brainstorm with another model, or a model of its own with the **Brainstorming** tag in **AI Integrations**.
