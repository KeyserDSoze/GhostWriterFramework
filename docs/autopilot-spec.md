# Autopilot and Director Spec

Narrarium can write a book on autopilot while the author keeps the final word. The same engine ([`narrarium-engine`](../packages/engine/README.md)) runs in the web app and in the personal server ([`narrarium-server`](../packages/server/README.md)), so the book files, the decisions and the live events are identical wherever it runs.

## Roles

- **Architect and ghostwriter** — design the concept and the plan, then write scene by scene following the selected ghostwriter profile.
- **Simulated readers** — read each finished chapter *blind*: no canon, no secrets, no plan. They write the same reader evaluations as the Reader evaluations page, then a panel summary.
- **Specialists** — check continuity, canon, secrets and reveal timing, timeline, character voice, style and pacing. Every finding must quote the text.
- **Director** — the author's stand-in. It approves or corrects the plan, decides on every finding (accept with a precise fix, or reject with a reason), scores the chapter and carries standing guidance forward. The software, not the model, turns accepted findings into repairs. When its autonomy allows it, the director asks the author instead of deciding a critical matter.

## Pipeline

1. Concept: story design, world context, characters, locations, factions, secrets with their reveal chapter.
2. Plan: chapters; simulated readers react to the pitch and the plan; the director approves or asks for changes. By default the autopilot pauses here so the author can read the plan.
3. For every chapter: scene plan → prose → readers → specialists → director → repairs → verification round (up to the director's limit) → consolidation (chapter summary, recap, story so far, `plot.md`).
4. Final: the director's judgement on the whole book.

A book that already has chapters is continued after the last one; chapters written by the author are never rewritten unless the author asks.

## Estimating a run

Before a run starts the interface can say what it will cost. `estimateAutopilotRun` (engine) is arithmetic over the pipeline above: it counts the calls each step makes, weighs each as the engine's router weighs its prompt, and prices the result with the models the author configured. It reads nothing and is closed-form, so a preview can be recomputed on every keystroke, also for a book of thousands of chapters. The details, the input, the formulas and how it was verified are in the [engine README](../packages/engine/README.md#estimating-a-run); this is the contract.

- **Calls.** For `C` chapters to write, `S` scenes, `R` readers, `r` rounds of review, `p` scenes repaired in a chapter and `v` revisions of the plan: one concept (unless the book has prose or a plan); one summary for each chapter the author wrote without one; `⌈chapters to plan / 20⌉ × (1 + v)` calls of the plan, `1 + v` reviews of it, and `R` readers on it for a fresh plan; then per chapter one scene plan, `S` scenes, `S + p` summaries, `R` readers and their summary, `r` calls of the specialists (one for all of them), `r` of the director, `p` repairs and one closing; one final judgement. Capabilities: `autopilot` (concept, plan, scenes, scenes' prose, repairs, closing), `director`, `reader-evaluation`, `reader-evaluation-summary`, `review` (the specialists) and `simple-tasks` (the summaries).
- **An interval, not a figure.** The reviews are the director's: from the least (`r = 1`, `p = 0`, `v = 0`) to the most (`r` the director's limit, `p = r × S`, `v = 2`). The ends are bounds, not a typical run; a host that knows what the reviews will be pins them and gets one number.
- **Tokens.** Prompts are counted as the router counts them (four characters a token, with the real system prompts and functions), the text of the book at 1.5 tokens a word; replies at `tokensPerWord(language)` a word (2.4 for the languages in Latin script, more for the others), within the limits the pipeline sets. The sizes the estimate cannot know (the files of the book, the length of a reply) are typical and named (`ESTIMATE_TYPICAL`); the host passes what it has measured.
- **Money and time.** Cost is the tokens of each capability at the price of its model, `null` where a model has no price (`unpriced` says which); the cache counts at its price in the best case only. Minutes are the tokens the models write at their speed, `null` where a speed is not known: it is never made up. `measuredSpeed` takes it from the log of activities.
- **Before it starts.** Warnings with stable codes (`noModel`, `smallWindow`, `smallOutput`, `noPrice`, `noSpeed`, `reasoningModel`, `outlineLongerThanBrief`) and the list of what was assumed come in the language of the book. A window smaller than the largest prompt of a step is an error: the router would refuse the prompt.
- **Starting points.** `AUTOPILOT_PRESETS` (`story`, `novella`, `novel-draft`) are the skeletons of a NEW book: `autopilotPresetSkeleton(id)` gives the chapter entries its outline starts with (4, 12, 24), the words of a scene and the review options. They are not an autopilot setting: once the book exists, its outline and its brief say the rest. A book that is started, or one that grows (chapter entries added to the Synopsis), counts only what is left.

## Author interventions

| Scope | Effect |
|---|---|
| Next step only | Delivered once to the next writing step, then marked as applied. |
| From now on | A permanent rule for every following step, the director included. |
| Revise what is already written | The director picks the scenes that must change; they are rewritten and reviewed again. |

An intervention can interrupt the running step, which is then repeated with it. The author's instructions always win over the director's taste.

## Retries

A step that fails with a transient error of the provider is retried three times, after about 4 s, 12 s and 36 s. That is the quick level, and for a provider that comes back within a minute it is enough. A provider that stays down longer (a local model whose nodes restart, an endpoint overloaded at night: HTTP 503 and 500 for minutes) used to end the run, and a night of work was lost to a fault of a few minutes. **Retry later** («Riprova più tardi», `options.longRetry`) is the second level, taken from BookWriter's long-term retry and off unless the author turns it on:

- **The policy.** `{ everyMinutes, cycles }`: wait `everyMinutes` (1 to 10080, a week) after the quick retries are used up, then try the step again with all its quick retries, up to `cycles` times (1 to 1000, or `"unlimited"`, written as a word: an absent value is never consent). It is saved in the book with the other options, and it can change while a run goes on, also while the run waits for it.
- **Which failures wait.** Only the transient failures of the provider: HTTP 408, 429 and 5xx, an endpoint that cannot be reached, a timeout, a stream lost or ended too early, an empty reply. Never a reply the engine rejected, a configuration error (401, 400, Tools only, a prompt too large, a reply cut by its limit), another 4xx, a failure of the book store, a conflict, the budget or the author's pause: waiting would change nothing, so the run stops as it always did.
- **The wait belongs to the run.** The run keeps the book (its heartbeat goes on), nothing is saved for the wait (no plan of the next attempt, no step in the history for the time that passes), the cost of every failed attempt is counted, and the budget, the limits, a pause and the lease are checked before every step as always: a pause ends the wait at once, and a run whose budget is used up pauses for the budget instead of waiting. The cycles start again from zero after every step that works; when they are used up the run stops with the error and says after how many restarts. If the process dies (the tab that runs the book in a browser is closed, or the personal server restarts), the run is interrupted like any other, and resuming it repeats the step at once with the cycles from zero: in a browser the wait goes on only while the page is open, on the server it goes on with no page open.
- **Turned off while it waits**, the run stops at once with the failure it waited to try again, and says that «Retry later» was turned off; new minutes or cycles count from the next cycle.

**Retry now** («Riprova ora», `session.retryNow()`) ends a wait, quick or long, so that the attempt starts at once. It uses up nothing more (the retry or the cycle stays counted) and it is not a pause.

What an interface reads, all from the engine (see *Retries* and *What the interface reads* in the [`narrarium-engine` README](../packages/engine/README.md)): `summary.retry` while the run waits (`{ at, attempt, retries }`, with `longTerm: { cycle, cycles }` in a wait of Retry later, `cycles: null` for no limit), the insights `failureRetry`, `longRetryWait` (`cycle`, `cycles`, `waitMs`), `retrySkipped` (`waitMs`) and `longRetryExhausted`, the codes of `run-ended` `longRetryExhausted` and `longRetryOff`, and `options.longRetry`. On a personal server the same engine does the same, behind the feature `autopilot-retry`: `longRetry` in `PUT /books/:id/autopilot/options` and `POST /books/:id/autopilot/retry-now` (and the MCP tool `autopilot_retry_now`); the engine's client never sends `longRetry` to a server without the feature.

**In the web app.** **Retry now** is wherever the run waits, and only while `summary.retry` is there: in the error panel under the band, in the quick commands of the band and of the mini-player. In this browser it goes through the runtime to the engine's session of the tab that runs the book (another tab is asked over the channel of the tabs, as a pause is, and answers whether the wait was skipped); for a book on the personal server it is `retryNow(bookId)` of the engine's client, and a server without `autopilot-retry` shows no button (the error panel alone says why: the autopilot tries again by itself). What it came to is said in words, in a polite status that is there before it speaks: «Retrying now.», or that the wait was already over when nothing waited (`false`, which is no error). The button is busy, not disabled, while the command is on its way, so that it keeps the focus; when the wait ends and the button goes, the focus goes to the band (from the panel) or to the pause of the same bar. While the run waits the band says so instead of «Preparing the next step», in one line that is its status: «Waiting to retry · retry 2 of 3», or in a wait of Retry later «Waiting to retry · restart 2 of 3 · not before 14:35» (the time in the author's time zone, `Intl.DateTimeFormat`, and the day in words when it is not today); the mini-player says the same, with the time left of a quick wait in a timer of its own. The panel does not say the line again (nothing on the page is said twice): it keeps the two counters apart (quick retries, restarts), says not before when beside its countdown, and in a wait of Retry later offers **Retry now**, the pause the band has (it ends the wait and stops the run) and **Change settings** (`/app/books/:id/autopilot/settings#autopilot-retry`). The countdown is a `role="timer"` that is not announced: a polite status says when the wait begins and when it ends. In the feed and the timeline `longRetryWait` is a row of its own with a clock, told as a note and not counted as a problem, `retrySkipped` is a note, and `longRetryExhausted` is the end, a problem; a `run-ended` with `longRetryOff` says that in the language of the app. The error panel of a run that Retry later ended says how (the restarts used up, with how many when the notice told it, or turned off while it waited) from the `run-ended` of that run and, for a page opened after the run or another device, from the `code` the state keeps in `summary.lastError`, without the number of restarts, which only the notice has. A run that stopped on a failure Retry later would have waited for (`network`, `timeout`, `rate_limit`, or `provider` with a status 408, 429 or 5xx: `mayPassWithTime` in `autopilot/retry.ts`), with Retry later off and a host that has it, has a line in the error panel that says it can be turned on and leads to it; it never turns it on.

The setting is a card of the configuration, **Retries**, after the review: a switch that is off unless the author turns it on (also for a book written before it), with the consent in words always next to it and as its description (it is for this book; every new attempt may be billed, even when the earlier ones failed; only transient failures of the provider, never a discarded reply, a configuration or key error or the budget; the budgets still stop the run; in the browser the wait does not go on with the page closed, on the server it does); the minutes between restarts and the number of restarts, or «Keep going until it works or I stop it» chosen in words; **Use recommended values** (every 10 minutes, 6 times). The numbers are read as the engine reads them (`readWhole` and `readRetryLater`: whole numbers in range, nothing rounded or cut, an empty field is never «no limit»); a value it would refuse is said under its field (`aria-invalid`, `aria-describedby`) and nothing is sent: the focus goes to the first field with a problem. It goes with `configure` and `start` like the other options (`null` to turn off a policy the book had, and nothing at all while it stays off), and with `updateOptions` while a run goes on; the card says beforehand that turning it off while the run waits for it stops the run with the error. On a personal server without `autopilot-retry` the switch is off and cannot change, and says why.

## Asking the director

On any book, chapter or scene page, **Ask the director** returns a structured opinion: verdict, score, strengths, concerns with quoted evidence, decisions, next steps and, when asked, a direct answer. Opinions can be kept in the book.

### The director page

`/app/books/:bookId/director` is a character sheet over `director.md` (its format does not change): who the director is (name, language, persona), how it judges (autonomy, `strictness` and `approval_score` as sliders of half a point with words under them, `aria-valuetext` and a field for the number, `max_review_rounds` as a choice of 1-3), what it cares about (priorities, taste, red lines, reader weights) and its vision. Beside the values a preview says in words what the engine does with them, from its rules and nothing else (`director/sheetModel.ts`, pure): an approval under `approval_score` is withheld and the chapter revised, unless no fix can be applied (`runDirectorReview`; the plan review and an on-demand opinion apply the same rule); after `max_review_rounds` the chapter is closed with the last accepted fixes (`advanceState`); `autonomy: full` never asks and `ask-always` pauses after the plan and after every chapter; `strictness` is only a line of the director's prompt (`directorPrompt`), so its effect is said not to be computable. `language` is chosen among the codes the engine knows (`TOKENS_PER_WORD`), the book's first and the default of a director not saved yet; a value the engine does not know is kept as written. The engine does not read it: opinions are written in the language of the book. A sticky bar saves or discards the sheet (it publishes its height as `--bottom-reserve`, so the focused field scrolls clear of it); leaving with changes asks (`useDirtyNavigationGuard`). An opinion is a score card (the score large with its scale, the verdict, strengths and concerns, decisions, next steps, cost, tokens and model when the provider said them). The saved opinions (`listDirectorOpinions`: one per target, the last one) are compact score cards, newest first, and a trend by chapter or by date: a plain SVG with the approval score of now as a dashed line, `role="img"` with its figures in words, and a table with every figure. `DirectorOpinionRecord` has no cost, so a saved opinion shows none.

## Files

| Path | Content |
|---|---|
| `director.md` | Director profile: `type: director`, `name`, `persona`, `autonomy` (`full`, `ask-on-critical`, `ask-always`), `strictness`, `approval_score`, `max_review_rounds`, `priorities`, `taste`, `red_lines`, `reader_weights`, and the vision in the body. |
| `autopilot/state.json` | Brief, options, plan, current review cycle, author rules and interventions, director guidance, statistics. |
| `autopilot/activity.json` | The last 200 steps: what, why, next, result, model, tokens and cost. |
| `autopilot/plan.md` | Human-readable mirror of the plan. |
| `evaluations/director/` | Director opinions (`type: director-opinion`) for the book, chapters and paragraphs. |
| `evaluations/readers/` | Simulated reader evaluations and panel summaries. |
| `outline/book.md`, `outline/chapters/<NNN>.md` | The Synopsis (the outline; once called the Scaletta): the idea, promise or pitch of the book (its description, which is also the brief's idea) and one entry per chapter (hypothetical title, what happens, ghostwriter, `origin`). See *The Synopsis*. |
| `outline/_brief.md` | The brief of the autopilot: `genre`, `audience`, `wordsPerScene` in the frontmatter, the chapter-by-chapter plot (`synopsis`) as the body. The idea is the description in `outline/book.md`, not a key of this file. Not a chapter: see *The brief* below. |

Chapters, paragraphs, canon entries, resumes and `plot.md` use the normal Narrarium formats and pass `validate_book`/`doctor_book`. Run `sync_plot`, `sync_all_resumes` and `sync_story_state` to regenerate the deterministic maintenance files after an autopilot session.

## The Synopsis

The Synopsis is a main part of the book (book menu → **Synopsis**, `/app/books/:bookId/outline`; «Sinossi» in Italian): the author's chapter-by-chapter plan, which the autopilot follows as binding and fills from its plan where it has no entry (`origin: autopilot`, with the `digest` of what it wrote). The page and the Copilot read it with the engine (`parseOutline`) and change it only through `outlineChanges`, composed into one commit that expects every file as it was read (a version that changed meanwhile is never overwritten: the page keeps the author's text and offers **Reload**). Every entry they write is the author's (`origin: author`, no `digest`), so the autopilot never rewrites it.

- **Saving.** Explicit: each entry has its **Save** (and **Save all**, Ctrl+S); nothing is written while the author types, because the autopilot plans a chapter again as soon as its entry changes. Structural actions (add, insert before/after, move up/down, delete) are immediate commits; the last action has an exact **Undo** (the files as they were, each expecting the version the action left). The page saves through the engine's `BookStore` over the local working copy, like the Director page and the browser autopilot, so the commit is published like any other and reaches a book that runs on the personal server with the usual sync.
- **What it shows.** Per entry, in words and with an icon: written by the autopilot and untouched (`origin: autopilot` and `intact`), the author's, chapter already written (a chapter folder with scenes, with its link), frozen chapter (its `chapter.md` holds a record `parseChapterFreeze` reads; only the chapters of the 50 entries on screen are read), chapter approved (`PlannedChapter.status: done`), and "changed after the plan" (the entry's `contentDigest` differs from `PlannedChapter.outlineDigest` of a chapter not written yet); the engine's warnings on the file, and a ghostwriter that does not exist. The entry of a written chapter whose plan remembers the entry (an `outlineDigest`) says, by its **Save** and again in the announcement of the save, what the autopilot will do with the change: the engine's rule (`changeOfWritten`, which the personal server's MCP tools tell too). A chapter the plan closed (`status: done`) is decided as `options.frozenEdits` says: ask whether to rewrite the chapter to follow the synopsis, rewrite it (never a chapter of the author, `imported`, which is only reported), or report it. A chapter the autopilot has in its hands (one of its scenes is past `planned` and the chapter is not closed, a revision rewrites it, or the decision about it is being carried out) is `later`: the change is told once and decided once the chapter is closed, whatever `frozenEdits` says by then. A chapter not written yet is planned again (the status «changed after the plan»), and says nothing more. The question that asks before an entry is deleted says what becomes of its chapter: one not written leaves the plan, one that is written and whose plan remembers the entry stays in the book and in the plan, and the run only says once that the entry is gone (`stays`).
- **Long books.** Windows of 50 entries, a filter (number, title, text) and a jump to a number; the files of `outline/` are read with one range query of the working copy.
- **Empty synopsis.** Add the first chapter, open the autopilot's Configuration (`/app/books/:bookId/autopilot/settings`), or convert the brief's chapter-by-chapter plot, `brief.synopsis` (lines `Chapter N: …` become entries, the rest the description) after a preview.
- **The context menu** of its text areas: improve and summarize the entry, **Develop this chapter** and **Check against the canon and the nearby chapters**, always with a preview and Apply/Cancel; applying is an unsaved change. Custom actions for the book appear there too.
- **The Copilot.** On the Synopsis and on the autopilot's pages (`book-outline`, `book-autopilot`, `book-autopilot-*`) «chapter N» is entry N of the outline; elsewhere it is the written chapter; «synopsis»/«sinossi»/«outline»/«scaletta» always mean the outline (`chapterTargetMode`, `resolveChapterReference`, written in the Copilot's instructions). On an outline page, a request about scenes or prose is a doubt and the Copilot asks. The tool **Edit the Synopsis** (`edit-outline`) proposes an `apply-file-updates` action made only of `outline/**` files: it may delete entries (`content: null`) and touch up to 100 files, and it is applied whole, after the author's confirmation, where the page saves (one commit in the local working copy, so it works on a book kept on the device and while the page's own saves wait to be published), and undone the same way.
- **Brainstorming.** The Copilot's mode for the big design of the book, entered only from the Synopsis page (its header and the launcher of the shell while the page is open) and ended by «Back to Copilot», the Copilot's own launcher or leaving the page (`useAssistantStore().brainstorming`; the Copilot's chat waits in `copilotSession`). Its code is `src/assistant/brainstorm/` and `src/components/assistant/brainstorm/`, loaded with the page or with the mode, never with the app or the panel. Its context is the Synopsis (`outline/book.md` and every entry), the description of `book.md` and the brief, the summaries and the state (approved, frozen, imported) of the written chapters, and the names of the canon without the secrets. Its registry is its own and has seven tools, `read_synopsis`, `read_synopsis_entry`, `read_chapter_summary`, `read_canon_entry`, `propose_synopsis_edits`, `check_synopsis` (the engine's warnings and `outlinePlanStatus`) and `save_brainstorm_notes`; any other call is refused in code and said to the model and the author. A turn is a JSON answer `{reply, calls}` of at most four rounds through the AI router with the Copilot's task (`copilot`), so it works with any model and costs what the Copilot costs. A proposal is the Copilot's own `apply-file-updates` of files of `outline/` (`toolId: edit-outline`, so «Edit the Synopsis» in the Copilot's tools allows or forbids both), composed by `composeOutlineEdits`, shown with the difference of every file and `groupOutlineChanges`, confirmed, and applied where the page saves. The notes go to `outline/brainstorm.md` (`OUTLINE_BRAINSTORM_FILE`), a dated section each time, a file of the outline that is never an entry. Its chats are the Copilot's chats with `mode: "brainstorming"` and their `bookId`, listed apart.
- **One composer, one rule.** A series of edits becomes one commit with the engine's `composeOutlineEdits` (the edits one after the other, each entry followed through the renumbering, only the files that are needed read: 5000 entries are no problem), and what the autopilot does when an entry changes (`ask`, `auto`, `notify`, said once while the chapter is written, planned again, left in the book, nothing) is `changeOfWritten`, pinned to what `outlinePlanStatus` and `settleFrozenChanges` do on a real book; see *Composing edits* in the [`narrarium-engine` README](../packages/engine/README.md). The personal server's MCP tools (`edit_outline`, `read_outline_entry`), the Synopsis page and the Copilot all use them. The page and the Copilot give the composer the files of the snapshot they already hold (`composerSource`), with no limit of edits or files and `missing: "warn"` (an edit of a chapter that has no entry changes nothing and a warning says so, as it always did); the page keeps what the author typed in an entry that moved with `remapOutlineNumber`, and groups the chapters it saves by what the autopilot does about each with `groupOutlineChanges`; the Copilot keeps its own limit of 100 files, and words each line of its proposal from the `steps` of the series, naming a chapter by the entry the edit is about (`origin`) even when an edit before it moved the numbers.
- **What the engine says.** The insights `outlineGhostwriter`, `outlineChanged`, `outlineRemoved`, `outlineWrittenChanged` and `outlineWarnings` are translated with their `chapterNumber` (and `ghostwriter`); the engine's own words stay for an event without the number. The engine says two more things with codes of their own: `outlineChangedWhileWriting` (the entry of a chapter still being written changed: the author decides once the chapter is closed) and `outlineRemovedWritten` (a written chapter is no longer in the synopsis: it stays in the book); `outlineWrittenChanged` is now only the change of the entry of a written chapter that `frozenEdits: "notify"` only reports. An app that does not translate the two new codes shows the engine's words. The step `replan` is "Replanning chapter N". The plan and the history name the ghostwriter of a chapter when it is not the book's (`PlannedChapter.ghostwriter`), and the plan lists `plan.removedChapters`. `options.fillOutline` is a switch of the settings (on unless the author turns it off). A stop for `evaluation-guidelines.md` (`profileProblems`) links to the Evaluation style page.

## Frozen chapters

The web app shows the engine's frozen chapters (see *Frozen chapters* in the [`narrarium-engine` README](../packages/engine/README.md)) and decides about them only through the engine: `session.frozenStatus`, `resolveFrozenChange`, `freezeChapter`/`unfreezeChapter` in the browser (a tab that does not run the book relays the command to the one that does), and the same routes on a personal server that announces `autopilot-frozen` (0.1.9), after a sync that hands the server the author's change; an older server is told to update, and `frozenEdits` is never sent to it.

- **The chapter and scene pages.** A bar with the record (frozen on the day, by the autopilot or by the author), **Freeze chapter** and **Unfreeze chapter** (which asks first). A change is found locally (`loadBookSnapshot` over the chapter's folder only, then `frozenChanges`): under the editor the page lists what changed and offers the actions of `frozenStatus` with what each one does; one that needs the model is queued (`state.pendingReviews`) and says when the autopilot carries it out, with **Start now** and a link to Live. A chapter the autopilot has in its hands, or that it has no plan for, says so.
- **Live.** The band (every size) and the mini-player say `summary.frozenChanges` («2 frozen chapters changed»); the page asks `frozenStatus` once when it opens on a book that is not running, so the count is there before a run. A question `frozen-change` or `outline-change` has its own heading, its `choices` as buttons labelled in the language of the app with what each does (the answer is the choice's `id`), no "accept" button, a link to the chapter or to the Outline, and a note by the text field: words of the author start a review that calls the model. The decisions in `state.pendingReviews` are listed in the order of the book, each saying whether it is in progress or waits for the next step or run, and whether the autopilot took it (`auto`).
- **Configuration.** **When you change a frozen chapter** (`options.frozenEdits`: ask every time, the default; review automatically; only report it), in **Review**, changes during a run too (`updateOptions`), and is disabled with the reason on a server without `autopilot-frozen`.
- **The Copilot.** A proposal (`apply-file-updates`, or the rewrite of a scene) that changes the text of a frozen chapter (its digests against the record, never a frontmatter alone) says what follows once applied, as `frozenEdits` says. A proposal that writes a whole `chapter.md` keeps the record the file holds, exactly, and drops one the model made up (`keepChapterFreeze`): a model never freezes or unfreezes a chapter, and only a proposal that deletes `chapter.md` says that it takes the freeze away. The chapters are read only when a proposal touches the text of one.
- **The insights** `frozenMigrated`, `frozenChanged`, `frozenAuto`, `outlineWrittenAuto`, `frozenReviewed`, `frozenCleared` and `frozenInvalid`, and the step `freeze`, are translated with their chapter number.

## Where it runs

- **In the browser**: each step is a local commit in the book's working copy; GitHub books are published as the author chose (every chapter unless chosen otherwise). Only one tab runs a book at a time; other tabs follow it live and can pause it, take a pause back, change the options that may change during a run, or send interventions.
- **On the personal server** (`npx narrarium-server`): the autopilot keeps working with the browser closed. GitHub books travel through GitHub (the server pushes after every step unless the author chose otherwise); local books are exchanged with a three-way file sync. The browser follows the server live.

### The Autopilot page

A control band stays at the top of the page and reads only what the engine already publishes: the status, the host and how long ago the run was last heard of (the newer of `summary.runner.heartbeatAt` and the last event the tab received), the step in progress with the text it streams (`live`; a step that answers with a value shows the JSON of its reply as it streams, and until it has some the row says «The model is answering… (12 KB)» from the `bytes` of `task-progress`, one report a second, in every layout of the band and shortened in the mini-player; in no live region), the progress (`summary.progress`), the phase, the steps of the chapter in progress (derived from the activity log and the review cycle: write, readers, specialists, director, fix) and the cost and token use against the budgets (`summary.stats`, `options.budgetLimit`, `options.budgetTokens`). Right under it come the things that wait for the author, each shown once: the director's question (or a question about a change of a frozen chapter or of the outline entry of a written one, with the decisions already queued), the error, the problems of a profile file, a plan to approve. The rest of the page is the history and, apart from it, the plan, the interventions, the rules (to read) and where it runs. The brief, the settings, the permanent rules to change and remove (with the text the author saw as `expectedText`, so that a rule changed in the meantime is refused with `rule_changed` and never overwritten; read-only on a personal server that does not announce `autopilot-rules`), the profiles it follows with their state and a danger zone with **Reset** (which asks first) are on a page of their own, the configuration (`/app/books/:id/autopilot/settings`), which saves with `configure` while the autopilot is stopped and, while it runs, only `publishEvery`, `pauseAfterEachChapter`, `frozenEdits`, `longRetry` and `resumeAfterRestart` with `updateOptions`. The history is one timeline made of what the web app already receives: the steps of the activity log (`AutopilotSnapshot.activities`, the last 200) and the events of the feed (`insight` and `sync` notices, which name their step with `activityId`; the `failureRetry` and `conflictRetry` notices, which name none; `run-started` and `run-ended`). It is grouped by chapter and by round of the review. The numbers of the rounds and their votes come from the plan (`state.plan.chapters[].rounds`, engine 0.1.6: the director's vote of each round, the readers' average of the first one, what the director accepted and rejected): the log keeps only the last 200 steps, so when a chapter has more recorded rounds than the log has reviews of it, the first ones are counted from the record, and a round has the vote and the verdict the plan recorded (`rounds[].verdict`, `approve` or `revise`, engine 0.1.8: what the round decided, which is not always what the model wrote, because an approval under the approval score of the director is withheld; the words of the review in the log are the fallback for a state that has none). A record that is shorter than the log's reviews of the chapter is not used (the page can hold a step of the log a moment before it has the plan that records it, and a vote on the wrong round is worse than none): the rounds are then numbered by the log and have what their reviews wrote. The verdict of a round is not in the record: it is read from the result line of the director's review (`approve 8/10 — …`) in one function, and a round whose review the log no longer has shows its vote and no verdict. For a state that has no record (an older engine), the round of a step is the number of director reviews of its chapter that finished before it, plus one (the closing of a chapter stays with the review that approved it). The group of the review of the book has the verdict and the score of `finalReview` (`summary.finalReview`; the log's `final` step is the fallback). A step that failed and was repeated is one row with its attempts (the activities that repeat a step that did not work: same kind, chapter, scene and reader), a retry notice belongs to the attempt that failed just before it, and a failed attempt says what kind of failure it was (`errorKind`) and which retry it belonged to (`errorDetail.attempt`, `errorDetail.retries`), also when the feed has no notice of it. The grouping lives in `autopilot/timelineModel.ts`, pure. The step that runs has no row (the band says what it is); its text, whole, is at the top of the list. The mini-player leaves out of its list what the page lists. The layout follows the width of the page and not of the window: two columns when it is wide; four tabs (Overview, Timeline, Plan, Rules) when it is not, with the Plan open while the plan waits for the author; on a phone the quick commands move to a bar at the bottom. A finished book offers to add chapters, and a card under the band, `AutopilotResult`, says how it went: the director's verdict on the whole book (`summary.finalReview`, whose words the page takes whole from `state.finalReview` when it is the same review; the log's `final` step is the fallback for a state that has none), the figures of the run (`summary.progress`, `summary.stats`, with the working time from `stats.workedMs`, the sum the engine counts over every run, and, for a book that was begun before the count (`stats.workedMsSince` is more than an hour after the state was created), «counted since» that day; without it the sum of the log, said to be a minimum when the log is full), how the chapters scored (the final score of each chapter and the votes of its rounds, from `state.plan.chapters[].rounds` (a round that sent the chapter back for fixes is marked with ↻ and said in words, when the plan records the verdict), in a picture with a text alternative and a table that a screen reader can read, windowed for a long book, with the lowest scores first on request), the three chapters to read first (the lowest final scores and the chapters that needed many rounds, each with the reason in a line) and the ways to read, export, ask the director and add chapters. What a state does not have (a verdict, a time, the rounds) is not shown and not made up. `autopilot/resultModel.ts` and `autopilot/bandModel.ts` are pure.

Which candidate of the AI Router the call of the step in progress asks now is one line (`AttemptNow`, `band/Attempt.tsx`) in the band, in the mini-player (the card of every book whose autopilot is active, and the tab of the book) and in the card of the step that runs in the history: the badge `Primary` or `Fallback N` (amber and with an arrow: the words and the arrow say it, not the colour alone), the model, the integration, `Thinking: High` (`Thinking: automatic` when no level is chosen, `Thinking: not sent` when the model does not take the one that was) and, for a call that went on to another request, `attempt 2/3`. It reads `live.trying`, which the engine says with the `call-attempt` event as each request is sent (engine 0.1.18; `applyLiveAttempt`, the second rule of `live.ts`, next to `applyLiveProgress`), so a primary that failed at once is already the fallback on screen. In the full band it is the line just before the cost line; in the other layouts it is under the step, in two lines at most on a phone; once shown it keeps its line between two steps, so that nothing under it jumps. A host that does not say it (a personal server older than the engine that does) shows nothing: no placeholder and no error. A tab that opens in the middle of a step of a run that belongs to another tab asks that tab (`live-request` and `live` on the channel of the tabs: `seedLive` keeps the answer only while the tab follows no step) and a run on the personal server says it in `snapshot().live`. The polite status says a call that went on to a fallback, once for each fallback of a step and never the primary that every call asks first (`useStreamAnnouncements`, at most one message every ten seconds). Next to the total cost the line shows what the step has spent so far when its activity says it (`stepSpendOf` in `autopilot/bandModel.ts`): the engine says what a step cost when it ends and nothing before, so today only the total is shown, and the costs dashboard changes that one function and nothing else.

A run that ends with an error, and a run that waits to try a failed step again, have one panel under the band, `ErrorPanel`. It reads `summary.lastError` (`kind`, `detail`, the message) for a run that ended, and `summary.status === "running"` with `summary.retry` (`at`, `attempt`, `retries`) for one that waits; the kind (`network`, `auth`, `rate_limit`, `timeout`, `truncated`, `refused`, `conflict`, `budget`, `limit`, `profile`, `config`, `provider`, `unknown`) gives the title, the explanation and the actions, `detail` says where it happened (the endpoint, the model, the provider, the integration by the name the settings give it, the status, which retry), and the step and the number of attempts that failed in a row with the same words come from the log of the steps and the events the page heard: the step is the activity whose id `lastError.activityId` names (engine 0.1.8; when the log does not have it there is no step, and none is guessed from the clock), and a state without the id is matched by the time of the error, as it always was. `detail.route` (engine 0.1.17: `AutopilotRouteStep[]`, every candidate of the route of the call that failed, in the order the author wrote it, each asked with its outcome, status, duration and words or left out with `leftOut`, `why`, `promptTokens` and `windowTokens`) is listed by `RouteTrail` when it says more than the facts do (several candidates, or one left out): the endpoint, the model, the provider and the integration above are the last candidate's, so they are left out of the facts then; the page names the integration by the name the settings give it. A `timeout` whose last asked candidate says `routing` ran out of the routing time that was left (`routingExecution.maxTotalDurationMs` less what the candidates before it took), which the author raises in the AI Router, not of the four hours. A book on the personal server adds `ServerAiNote`: the server asks with the AI configuration that was sent to it (`GET /api/v1/ai` says how many integrations, since when and its routing), and `PUT /api/v1/ai` sends the current one. Two details are said beside the facts, outside the alert, because they can differ from one attempt to the next: for a `timeout`, which limit ran out (`timeoutPhase`: waiting for the response to begin, silence between two pieces of it, or the total limit of 4 hours) and how long it was (`timeoutMs`), with the name of the field of the integration that changes it («Provider timeout», «Idle timeout»; the total limit cannot be changed); and, after a 429 or a 503, how long the provider asked to wait (`retryAfterMs`), next to the countdown. A browser can read `Retry-After` from another origin only when the provider exposes it (`Access-Control-Expose-Headers`); the personal server always can. A `config` error (engine 0.1.8: «Tools only» is on and no model of the step can call a function, so nothing was sent) says so in the alert, with the models that were left out, leads to **AI Integrations** and to the **Tools only** switch of the AI Router (`/app/settings/ai-router#tools-only`), and offers no **Retry now** while `toolsOnlyReport(settings)` still finds a task without a model that can call functions: the same configuration would give the same error. The own **Retry** of the band is the same for every kind, and it is not touched: it fails at once, with nothing sent. For `network`, `auth`, `timeout` and `provider` (`offersConnectionCheck(kind)`, pure, in `autopilot/errorModel.ts`; `errorActions` is not changed) the panel also offers **Check the connection** on the integration the failed step names (`errorDetail.integration`), when this browser has it and it is OpenAI-compatible: the same `probeEndpoint` check, with the same status line and the same words as the card of the integration, and **Open this integration** (`/app/settings/ai-integrations#integration-<id>`, which opens that card, brings it into view and focuses its title). The check asks from the browser even when the run is on the personal server, and the panel says so; the result belongs to the failure, so another failure forgets it. A state that an older engine wrote has no kind: the panel is an error with the words as they were, and the refusal to rewrite a file whose front matter cannot be read is read from the words as a profile problem. The countdown is counted from `retry.at`, the engine's clock, in a `role="timer"` that is not announced and outside the `role="alert"`; a polite status says when the wait begins and when it is over. The retry goes away with the `state` event that has none (the page keeps the newest summary it has heard, whole). **Copy diagnostics** copies a short English text with the category, where it happened, the message with no key and no path of a disk, the versions of the app and of the engine and the time. The mini-player and the list of books keep a short notice with the kind of the failure and, in the mini-player, the way to the page. `autopilot/errorModel.ts` is pure.

The mini-player of the web app (the Autopilot button next to Copilot) shows the state, the step in progress, the progress and the commands of one book, and in its "Live" tab one line for each of the latest events of every book. It keeps out of the way on the Autopilot pages, where the page shows all this, and keeps clear of the buttons and the fields of the assistant on a phone.

### The start form: sizes and the preview of the run

The configuration page (`/app/books/:id/autopilot/settings`, `SetupCard`) starts the brief with a choice of three sizes and ends the part about the book with an estimate of the run. Neither writes anything of the book.

- **Sizes.** `AUTOPILOT_PRESETS` (`story`, `novella`, `novel-draft`) are the starting points offered when a NEW book is created: the site calls `autopilotPresetSkeleton(id)` for the chapter entries to create, the words of a scene and the review options, and for nothing else. The autopilot's form has no preset, no chapter count and no scene count: the length of the book is its outline (`Chapter N` entries), the scenes of a chapter are the planner's, and `wordsPerScene` is the only number of the brief.
- **The brief.** The idea of the book is the description in the Synopsis (`outline/book.md`, «Idea, promise or pitch»): stored there and nowhere else. The rest of the brief (genre, audience, words per scene, the chapter-by-chapter plot) is in `outline/_brief.md`. `readOutlineBrief(store)` composes both into one brief; `writeOutlineBrief(store, fields)` writes the idea and the fields in one commit, compare-and-swap on both files. An idea over 1 000 000 characters is refused (413 `text_too_long` on the routes), never cut. `POST …/autopilot/configure` and `POST …/autopilot/start` accept `brief` and write the file before the run reads it. The file is never a chapter: `write_file` refuses `outline/`, `edit_outline` never deletes, renumbers or moves it, and `read_outline` and `autopilot_status` show it as the book's brief (idea, genre, audience, words per scene, a synopsis excerpt, the number of chapter entries). A book with no chapter entry stops the run with «The synopsis has no chapters: write the chapters in the Synopsis, then resume.» A state saved before this rule (with `targetChapters` and `scenesPerChapter`) runs to the end of its own plan, and a completed book is extended only when the author adds chapter entries. A legacy idea in a state is written into `outline/book.md` once, in the start commit, when the book has no description of the author's (an author's description is never overwritten).
- **The preview** («What to expect from this run»). `estimateAutopilotRun` is called in memory, a short pause after the last edit (350 ms: one calculation for a burst of keys, and the field never waits for it), with the form as it is now, saved or not. Where each input comes from: `models` is `estimateModelsFromConfig(config, measuredSpeed(activities, { ignoreTasks: TWO_CALL_TASKS }))` (the speed comes from the log of the book, and the minutes stay `null` until it has run); `maxReviewRounds`, `readerPanelSize` (the enabled readers the director does not give a weight of zero), the words of `story-design.md`, the world context, the ghostwriter the writer uses (`ghostwriterPrompt`), the director (`directorPrompt`) and the evaluation style (`evaluationStyleBlock`) are read from the files of the book in one batch; a story design or a world context that is still the scaffold is not measured (the concept writes it first) and `conceptDone` is true for a book whose design is written (`!isThinDocument`), that has a plan, or whose state says so; `outline` is `{ entries, lastChapter, words }` of the entries the author wrote (`parseOutline`, `intact` entries are the autopilot's own and do not count) read as the folder `outline/`; `progress` is `estimateProgressFromState(state)` for a book with a plan and `estimateProgressFromChapters` of its chapters (a chapter with a resume file counts as summarized) for one without, so that a started book counts only the rest and «Add chapters» only the new chapters; `rulesWords` and `notesWords` come from `estimateContextFromState`, and the canon is counted from the lists of the book (the engine's typical manifest of ten entries) without reading its files, which the assumptions say. When the files of a book cannot be read (a book whose working copy this browser does not have) the engine's typical sizes are used and the page says so. The same estimate, for a plan that waits for the author, is made by `estimateRest` (see *The approval of the plan*).
- **After a run.** `tokensPerWord` is measured on the book: for the latest scenes (`write` steps whose summary says `N words`) the median of `usage.outputTokens` over those words, from three scenes on. A step of writing is the scene and then its summary, so the figure is a little high, on the prudent side. The page says that the time and the tokens are based on what the book did, and from how many steps. (The words of a scene are read from the summary of its step; a field of the activity would be firmer.)
- **Where the models come from.** A book that runs in this browser is estimated with this browser's AI configuration (`pickAiRuntimeConfig(settings)`), which makes the calls. A book that runs on the personal server is estimated with the server's: `GET /api/v1/ai` (`ServerAiStatus`: integrations with the window and the limits each model declares, and `taskRouting`) is read through the verified connection once the server is online, and made into an `AiRuntimeConfig` (`configFromServerStatus`); its safe view carries no key and no price, so a model is priced with the browser's price for the same integration id and the same model name, and a model the browser does not know has no price (the cost is then not known). A server with no AI configuration has no model for any role (`noModel`); a server that cannot be asked leaves this browser's configuration in its place, and the page says so.
- **What it shows.** The chapters and words (only the rest for a book that started), calls (in all and by role: writer, director, readers and their summary, specialists, summaries, each with its model), tokens sent and written, cost and minutes. Figures are ranges rounded outward to two significant figures (`roundRange`): what is shown always holds what the engine computed and no digit says more than it knows; numbers are formatted with `Intl.NumberFormat` in the language of the page. A cost that is `null` says «Not known» and for which models, with the part the priced roles add up to; minutes that are `null` say «Not estimated» and why. Warnings come from the engine with their codes: `noModel` (one line for all the roles that have none) and `smallWindow` are problems with the way to the row of the role in the AI Router (`/app/settings/ai-router#router-row-<task>`, which takes the focus), to AI Integrations when there is none, or to the settings of the personal server for a book that runs there; `noPrice`, `noSpeed` and `reasoningModel` are one line for each model, naming its roles; `smallOutput` and `outlineLongerThanBrief` keep the engine's words (in the language of the book, marked with `lang`). Every warning has an icon and a word (Problem, Warning, Note), never only a colour. The assumptions of the engine are in a section that opens and closes.
- **For a screen reader.** The figures are not in a live region: they change after a pause, not under the reader's hand. A status, polite and out of sight, says the outcome (chapters, calls, cost, time, problems) once, a longer pause (1.2 s) after the figures moved, and only when something was changed by the author and the outcome differs from the one already told.
- **No empty cards at rest.** A book that never ran has no card for the history (`AutopilotTimeline`: one line says that every step will appear there once the autopilot starts) and the feeds of events (the live panel, the Copilot tab, the overview) are one line of text without a frame.

### The page of every book

`/app/autopilot` has a card for every book, and reads each book as it always did: the runtime's download of `autopilot/state.json` and `autopilot/activity.json` (or the personal server's `GET …/autopilot`), four books at a time, with `ensureServerStream` for the live summaries. A card reads only what the store already holds: the summary (`latestSummary`: the last `state` event, else `summarizeAutopilotState` of the saved state), the state for the plan, the options and the votes, the log for the last step and the step that runs (`live`). The ring and the words say the chapters closed out of the ones planned (`progress.chaptersDone`/`chapters`); the state is one of: never started, running, waiting to try again (`retry`), paused, plan to approve (`plan.awaitingAuthor`), waiting for an answer (`question`), profile files to correct, stopped by an error, finished; the director's last vote is `finalReview.score`, else the last vote of the chapter of the plan with the highest number that has one (`rounds`, with the readers' average of its first round); the cost is `stats.cost` against `options.budgetLimit` (and the tokens against `budgetTokens`). The actions are the quick commands of the book (`QuickCommands` variant `hub`: what runs, without publishing and the host, which are on the book's page), with "Open the result" for a finished book and "Start autopilot" for a state that never ran; "Open Live", and "Set up and start" for a book without a state. Books that ask the author something come first (an answer, a plan to approve, profile files, an error, frozen chapters that changed), then the ones that run, then the others, in the order of the library; the order is worked out from a string of one character per book, without summarizing any state, so the text a run streams draws again only its own card. A filter by title (any script, `matchKey`) appears from eleven books. A book that cannot be read has a card that says so, with a retry. The global feed is collapsible (closed on a phone, the choice kept in `localStorage`), and does not repeat the step that runs.

### The approval of the plan

While the plan waits for the author (`plan.awaitingAuthor`, and nothing runs), the live page shows the approval in place of the history (`PlanApproval`; in the Plan tab when the page has tabs), and the band and the notices under it say nothing more about it: the plan is ready is said once. What it shows comes from the saved state and the log of the steps: the brief's idea, the logline of the concept (`concept.logline`), the director's reviews of the plan (the `plan-review` steps of the planning that led to it, read back from their result line, `approve 8/10 — …`: the engine keeps the vote and the words of a review of the plan nowhere else) and the revisions it asked for (`plan.rounds`), the readers' reactions (`plan.readerReactions`: name, interest, reaction, concerns, wishes; `plan.skippedReaders`), and every chapter of the plan, open (summary, goal, point of view, key events, reveals, scenes, `ghostwriter` when it is not the book's, `imported`). Three things are read from the book, only the files that hold them: the premise (the section of `story-design.md` called so, else its first section), the cast (the files under `characters/` that the last concept step wrote, `changedPaths`, one line each from `function_in_book` and `story_role`) and who wrote the entry of the outline of the chapters on the screen (`origin` and `digest`, read as the engine reads them: `intact` is the autopilot's). A long plan is shown twenty chapters at a time, with a search and a jump to a number.

«Ask for a change» on a chapter is the intervention that rewrites (`intervene`, `scope: "rewrite"`), with the author's words prefixed by the chapter («Chapter 3 «Title»: …»): no new mechanism. Its effect is the engine's: the words become a rule (`constraints`), and when nothing of the book is written the plan is reopened with them (`approved: false`, `awaitingAuthor: false`, the words added to `revisionNotes`); in a book with scenes written (chapters of the author) a revision of the written chapters starts instead, and the plan stays as it is. The field says which. A plan reopened with changes and no run (`planReviewMode` = `revise`: not approved, not awaiting, notes that are not only the extension of the book, status paused or idle) keeps the screen, which lists the changes and goes on with «Plan again with these changes» (a start); the band then does not repeat the engine's message of the plan that was ready, which the intervention does not clear. Approving is the quick command (a start), in the band at the top (on a phone in the bar at the bottom, which reserves its room so that it never covers the text) and again at the end of the plan. **What the rest will cost** (`PlanRestCost`) is at the end of the screen, between the sentence that says it is the whole plan and the button that approves it: the estimate of the start form's preview, for the plan as it is. `estimateRest(sources, state)` calls `estimateAutopilotRun` with the same models, director, outline and files as the preview (`useRunEstimateSources`) and with what the state has: `restBrief(state)` (`targetChapters` is the number of chapters the plan has, not the brief's; `scenesPerChapter` is the rounded average of the scenes the plan has planned for the chapters that are left, and the brief's number for the ones that have none yet, because `runScenes` plans them just before the chapter is written; the rest of the brief is as it was saved), `state.options`, `estimateProgressFromState(state)` (the chapters done, `planned` for an approved plan, the concept done, the chapters of the author with no summary, so that only what is left to write is counted) and `estimateContextFromState(state)`. The chapters the author's outline lost before they were written (`plan.removedChapters`) are taken off the last chapter of the outline, as `effectiveTargetChapters` takes them off the length of the book, so that the outline does not extend the book to replace them; an entry of the outline beyond the plan still does, and the extension is planned and counted. A plan that is to be made again (`planReviewMode` = `revise`, not approved) counts the new plan and its reviews, and says so. A plan with no chapter left to write has no card. The card waits for the screen to be drawn (`useAfterFirstPaint`: the next frame and the task after it), for the working branch and for the files, and then works out one estimate in memory for each `state.version` (a copy of the same state, or the log read again without a new step, is not a new calculation); the first draw reads nothing and asks the personal server nothing. It reads only what the preview reads (the director, the story design, the world context, the evaluation style, the ghostwriter's profile and the persona files, in one batch, and `outline/` as a folder), never the book; for a book that runs on the personal server it asks `GET /api/v1/ai` once, as the preview does, and says when the server did not answer. Four figures (calls, tokens in all, cost, time) are rounded outward to two figures and written with `Intl.NumberFormat` in the language of the page; `Not known` and `Not estimated` say why (`useWhyUnknown`, shared with the preview: a model with no price, no speed measured on the steps of the book yet, or, when no model is set at all, `noModelWhy`, which does not blame a price nobody could set). The figures are in no live region and nothing is announced. The problems are the ones that stop a run or cut it short, `restProblems(estimate.warnings)` (`REST_WARNING_CODES`: `noModel`, `smallWindow`, `smallOutput`): each is said once for what it is about and not once for each capability the engine counted it for (a small model asked for every role is one problem of its window and one of its replies, with the roles, the largest prompt and the longest reply), in the words of the page (`autopilot.approval.rest.*`; the engine's own sentences are not used here), as a Problem for what stops the run and a Warning for a window that fits with little to spare and for replies that would be cut. Each has its way: the row of the AI Router of the role (`#router-row-<capability>`; a button for each role when one or two are concerned, one for the first of the rows when there are more), AI Integrations when there is none, the settings of the personal server for a book that runs there. The other warnings (no price, no speed, a model that reasons, an outline longer than the brief) stay in the preview of the configuration, which a link opens (`/app/books/:id/autopilot/settings#autopilot-estimate`). The parts the card shares with the preview (`RangeText`, `Stat`, `WarningBox`, `EstimateWarnings`, `useWhyUnknown`) are in `components/autopilot/config/EstimateParts.tsx`. `autopilot/planApprovalModel.ts` is pure.

### The map of the chapters

The live page has a map of the chapters (`ChapterMap`) beside the history, with the plan, and on the page of a finished book under its result; it gives way to the approval while the plan waits. Every chapter is a row and every scene a cell. The state of a scene is one of seven, worked out from the saved state only: to write, being written (the step that runs, `write`, `repair` or `revise`, on that scene), written, in review (its chapter is), approved, to rewrite (in the `repairQueue` of the review in progress, or a target of a revision of the book still ahead of its `cursor`) and frozen; each has a symbol, a colour and words (the legend, the cell's own text for a screen reader, the table, the panel). A row says the state of its chapter, its final vote (`PlannedChapter.score`, or the vote of its last round, marked, while it is in review), how many rounds of review it had (`PlannedChapter.rounds`, persisted since engine 0.1.6), whether it is frozen (its `chapter.md` holds a record: read for the approved chapters of the window only) and whether it follows an entry the author wrote in the outline (the entries of the window). The map is a WAI-ARIA grid with one cell in the order of the Tab key (the arrows, Home and End, with Control to the ends of the map, Page Up and Page Down by ten rows, Enter and Space to open), and a table with the same words is one choice away (kept in `localStorage`). A click or Enter opens the panel of the chapter, a dialog that gives the focus back when it closes: the rounds (a picture of the votes of the director and of the readers, with a text alternative, and a table), the round in progress for the chapter in review (`state.review`: the readers' scores with their main problem and priority, the findings with their gravity, scene and source, the director's decisions and verdict), the scenes, what the plan says, and the ways to the chapter, to its readers' evaluations and to the director's opinions. The engine keeps the findings and the decisions of a round only while its chapter is in review (`state.review` is cleared when it closes; `PlannedChapter.rounds` keeps the votes and the counts only): for a closed chapter the panel reads, when asked, the director's last opinion the book keeps (`evaluations/director/chapters/<slug>.md`, one file). The map is drawn from `plan.chapters`, fifty chapters at a time (a search and a jump to a number, as the outline has), never from the book, and the text a step streams does not draw it again. `autopilot/chapterMapModel.ts` is pure.

### Quick commands

The web app puts the commands the author reaches for most in one bar (the control band of the Autopilot page, which becomes a bar at the bottom of the screen on a phone; the "This book" tab of the mini-player; and its "Live" tab for the book in focus). They all go through the engine, once, so the browser and the server behave the same:

- **Pause** `now`, `after-step` or `after-chapter`, and **cancel** a pause that waits (`session.pause`, `session.cancelPause`; `summary.pausePending` says what waits).
- **Run one step or one chapter**: a start with `limits: { steps: 1 }` or `{ chapters: 1 }`, for that run only (`summary.limits`).
- **Publish** never, every chapter or every step: `options.publishEvery`, which `session.updateOptions` changes during a run (`summary.publish` says what is in force). A browser publishes from both hooks, `afterCommit` (every step) and `afterChapter`.
- **Where it runs**: a handover plan per book, an intention of the tab that made it. It asks the run to pause (after the step, or now), waits until it has ended, moves the book (`moveToServer` or `moveToBrowser`: the same consents and conflict handling as "Where it runs"), and starts the autopilot on the other side only if it was running; with a number of steps it runs that many there (`limits.steps`) and comes back. A move that cannot be made leaves the book stopped where it was, with the reason; a failure after the move (the start on the other side, the way back) does not move the book back, and the message says where it is. A handover never sends a secret: a server that lacks the AI configuration or the book's GitHub token sends the author to "Where it runs".
- **Older servers** that do not announce `autopilot-controls` ignore `limits` and take `after-chapter` and `cancel` for `after-step`, so the web app does not send those commands to them and says which version of the server has them.

### The personal server

- **Pairing and identity**: the browser pairs with a nine-digit code generated in the server's admin panel (`http://127.0.0.1:24243`) and approved there by the owner. Before sending its token, the browser asks the server to sign a fresh nonce with its Ed25519 key and compares the key's fingerprint with the one remembered at pairing.
- **Permissions**: each client token carries `read`, `write`, `run` and, only when the owner allows it, `update`.
- **Updates**: a client with `update` (or the admin panel) can ask the supervisor to install the latest npm release. The supervisor installs it only when it carries the npm registry signature and the provenance attestation of this repository's publishing workflow, and only when no autopilot is running. A failed installation keeps the previous version.
- **Network**: the server listens on `127.0.0.1` only and calls only the AI endpoints approved by its owner. Remote access goes through HTTPS, for example the Cloudflare connector managed from the admin panel.
- **An older personal server**: a server that does not announce `autopilot-outline-brief` plans the default twelve chapters and ignores the Synopsis and the brief, and would spend the author's AI credits on them. The site therefore does not start or configure the autopilot of such a server: the configuration shows an alert (update the server, «Settings → Personal server»), and Start, Resume and Save are disabled with that reason. The engine's client refuses the requests for the same reason (`feature_unavailable`). Browser-hosted books and servers that announce the feature are unaffected.
- **Restarts**: when the server stops (Ctrl+C, a service stop, an error that stops it), every run pauses at once and its state says that the server stopped it (`stoppedBy: "server-shutdown"`, «In pausa perché il server si è fermato.»). With **Resume after a restart** on for the book («Riprendi da solo dopo un riavvio del server», `options.resumeAfterRestart`, off by default, in the «Retries» card of the configuration, also while a run goes on), the server resumes such a run by itself when it starts again, and a run left `running` by a process that ended without pausing it too: one book at a time, about five seconds apart, through every check a start of the author has (the AI configuration, the number of runs, the owner's switch and caps). At most three times a day per book (a private file of the server counts them, before each attempt): after the third the run stays paused, and its message says why. A run the author paused is never resumed, and a pause of the author takes the server's word away. A start that is refused leaves the run as it is and writes nothing to the book; the server's log says why. Each resume is a line of the admin log, and the run says it first in its feed (`resumedAfterRestart`). The web app shows the switch only for a server that announces `autopilot-auto-resume`.
- **Conflicts with GitHub**: a GitHub book on the server is a working copy that publishes the server's work and brings in the author's before every step. When both changed the same file (the author edits a chapter in the browser while the autopilot refines it on the server), the run stops with a `conflict` that says how many files (`errorDetail.githubConflicts`; `lastSync.conflicts`; a sync answers `409 github_conflict`), and nothing is lost. The author settles it the way the app already settles a conflict between a device and the server: for each file, or for all of them, **keep the server's version** or **take GitHub's**. The server lists the files (`listGithubConflicts`: a page at a time, each with what happened on each side and the size of each version, never what it says) and resolves them with one merge commit that keeps both histories (`resolveGithubConflicts`: choices per file and a default, the fingerprint of the list the author saw; it is refused, with nothing written, when a file has no choice or GitHub moved onto those files meanwhile), pushed when the book is published; the version not chosen stays in the history of the merge. The autopilot's own files (state, log, plan) are never asked about: the server's while its run is active, otherwise the side whose state was saved last, the three together. A file whose name the server does not write is shown as not resolvable here and kept as the server has it. The run does not start again by itself: the author resumes it as always. A server without the feature `github-conflicts` is told to update. See *Conflicts with GitHub* in the [`narrarium-server` README](../packages/server/README.md) for the rules of the server.

  The app settles them in one window (`GithubConflictsDialog`), which the page shows once (`GithubConflictsWindow`, opened through a small store by `GithubConflictsAction`, the button that the three places below offer), so that it outlives the control that opened it: the panel and the notice go away as soon as the conflicts are resolved, while the author is still reading what came of it. It is reached from three places that tell the author about the conflict. **The error panel**: a `conflict` whose `errorDetail.githubConflicts` has a count (`githubConflictsOfError`, pure) has its own title and explanation, and `errorActions(kind, { githubConflict: true })` makes resolving the main action and resuming the second, since resuming would only meet the conflict again; the other `conflict` (a file that changed while a step ran, no count) is untouched. Once the author has resolved them and not resumed yet, the panel says that they are resolved and makes resuming the main action again, instead of offering to resolve what is settled (`githubConflictsSettled`: the server's last sync worked after the error, which a resolution is, and so is any later sync that works; a server that forgot its last sync when it restarted gives no such sign, and the window then says that there is nothing left to resolve). **«Where it runs»**: the notice and the same button, while the server's summary of the book says its last sync stopped on conflicts (`lastSync.conflicts`, `githubConflictsOfSync`). **A manual sync**: a `409 github_conflict` from `syncGitHub` (a «Publish now», a «Sync now», the move of the book back to the browser) is kept in the summary of the book on the server (`noteGithubConflict`: `lastSync` with `conflicts`, as the server keeps it in its own, so the notice appears without waiting for the stream), is said in words with the number of files (`describeAutopilotError`) and, for «Sync now», is no longer left unsaid. A server whose `info` does not announce `github-conflicts` gets a note to update it, with the way to its settings, instead of the button, and nothing is sent to it (`assertGithubConflicts`: the runtime refuses with `feature_unavailable` before any request, as the engine's client does after asking the info); a server whose features are not known yet gets the button, and the window finds out.

  The window reads the list a page of fifty at a time (`runtime.githubConflicts`, which does not hold the book) and each page again when the author changes page; it holds the choices in memory (`Draft`: the version of each file, and a version `default` for all the others), so they stay while the author goes from page to page. «Resolve» is disabled until every file has a version (every page read and every file chosen, the files with `resolvable: false` needing none: they are listed apart, with why, and the server keeps its version) or a version was chosen for all the others, and asks before that default is applied to files the author did not choose one by one. The request carries the `fingerprint` of the list the author saw, the choices that differ from the default and the default; with more than 5000 choices the version most files have becomes the default and the others are the exceptions. `conflicts_changed` (GitHub moved again), and a later page whose fingerprint is not the one the author chose from, drop the choices and show the new list, saying so. Resolving is one operation of the book (`githubConflicts`: nothing else is started on it, and the window cannot be closed meanwhile), and afterwards the summary of the book on the server is read again. The author is never taken to Resume: the outcome (`resolved` counts, `unresolvable`, `ignored`, `autopilotFiles`, how it stands with GitHub: published, `pushError` («resolved on the server but not published; the next sync publishes it») or publishing off) offers it, with the focus on the summary, and the run is never started by itself. Every refusal has its words and the sensible action: `busy` (Pause, asked before anything is sent when the page knows the run is active), `choice_missing` (the first files), `not_conflicted` (Resume), `no_remote`, `unrelated_histories`, `invalid_github_path` (the server's sentence, in English, marked as such), `maintenance` (Try again), `git_failed`, `feature_unavailable`, an answer that did not come back (the author is not told that nothing was changed: the list is read again to see), the permission and the book that is not there. The texts are in a catalogue of their own, `githubConflicts` (`@/i18n/githubConflicts`), which travels with the views that show them.

See the [`narrarium-server` README](../packages/server/README.md) for commands, data folders and the release procedure.

## AI router tasks

The autopilot uses the router tasks **Autopilot** (writing), **Director**, **Reader evaluation**, **Reader evaluation summary**, **Review** (specialists) and **Simple tasks**. Tasks without a route fall back to models tagged `default`. For long scenes, set the context window and the max output tokens of the models.

**Tools only** (`settings.aiToolsOnly`; the switch is at the top of the AI Router page, and it is on unless the author turned it off) makes every agent of the autopilot answer through its own function: a model marked as without tool calls (**Forced tool calls** off, in the card of the integration) is skipped, and a reply in text instead of the call is refused. The Copilot obeys it too, for the structured answers it asks for (`completeToolRouted`: an evaluation; and the yes/no confirmation, which is simply unclear when no model can call its function): its prose is never affected, and when no model of the route can call a function the call ends with the engine's `AiToolsRequiredError`, which the chat shows with the explanation of the run, the sentence of the engine and a button to the switch (`assistant/aiFailure.ts`). The engine's own functions decide it (`pickAiRuntimeConfig`, `resolveAiCandidates`, `applySameBoundaryPolicy`), so the page does not repeat the rule: `router/toolsOnly.ts` asks them which tasks of the autopilot would skip a model or have none left, and the page says so under the switch and on the rows of those tasks. A task with no model that can call functions stops the run with a `config` error. Once the author has switched it the value is stored as a boolean, every copy of the settings keeps it (the cloud, the offline cache, the account replicas), the personal server receives it as `toolsOnly`, and it cannot be changed offline.

The **thinking** is chosen per route (a column of the text table) and per integration (**Default thinking**, in its card): the level of a route wins, *Automatic* sends nothing even when the integration has a level, and a route with no level takes the integration's. The **Default** row has no level of its own: it only chooses the endpoint of the tasks that have no route, so those tasks go out with the level of the integration, and the row says so and links to the card.

How a level reaches a model is the setting of the model, its **Thinking parameter** (`reasoningParameter` of the chat model, in the card of the integration next to **Forced tool calls**): `reasoning_effort` (the default, the OpenAI field), `chat_template_kwargs.reasoning_strength` (the argument of the chat template, which Muse Glimmer takes and llama.cpp reads in place of `reasoning_effort`) or `none` (no level is sent; it does not turn thinking off). The card suggests the chat-template form for the names the engine's `suggestedReasoningParameter` knows (Muse Glimmer today), only while the model has not said, and never applies it by itself; what is stored is exactly what the author chose, the default included. The engine, on the browser and on the personal server alike, and the site's own requests (the Copilot) follow the same word of the model, and a value this version does not know is kept as it is and not used.

The card of an OpenAI-compatible integration also asks its endpoint two things, with the address and the key as they are written (never for Azure OpenAI, GitHub Models or Microsoft 365, for which the engine answers `unsupported`; the GitHub catalog is its own). **Check the connection** is `probeEndpoint`: one `GET <base>/models`, and a status line (`role="status"`: «Responds in 120 ms · 6 models», «Responds with error 401: check the key», «Not reachable: …», which says that a browser cannot tell a server that is off from CORS) with the engine's English words in a disclosure. **Read the models from the endpoint** is `fetchModelCatalog`, in a dialog that is loaded when it opens with its own texts: the models of every kind (`/models`, and `/models/details` and `/modalities` when the gateway has them) grouped as Text, Images, Text-to-speech, Transcription, Embedding and Other, each with its window, its largest answer and its declared capabilities. The new text models are ticked, and none when the endpoint tells no kinds, because nothing says which of them chat. What is stored comes from `modelFieldsFromCatalogEntry` and is plain data of the model (`maxInputTokens`, `maxOutputTokens`, `supportsToolCalls`, `supportsStreaming`, `supportsReasoning`, `supportsVision`, which the engine reads from the model): a capability is stored only when it is `true`, because LlmProxy's `false` means «not declared», and a switch that was never set must not become a restriction. It never overwrites what the author wrote: a model that exists (same name, letter for letter) is filled only where a field is empty, a capability the author set, `false` included, stays as it is, and the roles, the names, the prices and the thinking parameter are never touched; a new model gets the declared fields, no role unless the integration has no model at all (the first one, the one that declares tool calls if any, takes the default roles), and the suggested `reasoningParameter` (`suggestedReasoningParameter`) only if the author leaves it ticked. A model that is not for chat can be set as the image, text-to-speech or transcription model of the integration while that field is empty. The rules are in `ai/catalogPlan.ts`, pure. The older **Load models from API**, in the list of chat models, is kept for the endpoints that answer with a bare list.

The **API version** of an Azure OpenAI integration is the default of its models, and each model may have its own (`ChatModel.apiVersion`, engine 0.1.19): each deployment may need another `api-version`. The card of a model says what it inherits in the placeholder of its field (`Inherits from the integration: 2025-01-01-preview`), writes the version the author types (trimmed; an empty field is no field) and shows a badge, `Own version`, when the model has one; the field of the integration says that it is the default of the models that have none; a value that cannot be a version (spaces, signs of an address) is said in words and with `aria-invalid`, in both fields. The rule is the engine's, in one place (`effectiveApiVersion(integration, model)`: the model's, else the integration's, else the default of the engine), and the client asks each candidate with its own, a fallback of another integration or model included. Image, speech and text-to-speech models are plain names on the integration, not model entries, so they keep its version. The version that was sent is said wherever an attempt is told: a fact and a line of the diagnostics of a failed step (`apiVersion` of the error detail), beside the address of each attempt of the router's trail (`AiRouteStep.apiVersion`), in the failed attempts of the records of a call and as a badge of the debug panel. The configuration that is sent to a personal server carries it (`pickAiRuntimeConfig`); a server that predates it keeps the version of the integration for every model.

The **API protocol** of a model says how its requests are made (`ChatModel.apiProtocol`, engine 0.1.20): Chat Completions, which is what no value means (a model saved before has no key, and is asked as it always was), or the **Responses API**, which takes a thinking level and function tools together (the models that refuse them on Chat Completions take them there). The **address of a model** (`ChatModel.endpointUrl`) is for a gateway whose address is not the one the engine builds from the integration: an http or https address with no user name or password, used exactly as it was written, query included, with the key of the integration in the header its provider expects (`api-key` for Azure OpenAI, a bearer token for the others); while it is set, no API version is used for that model, neither the integration's nor the model's, and the editor says so under the field and under the version of the model. Both are fields of the row of every model of an Azure OpenAI, OpenAI-compatible or GitHub Models integration (the select **API protocol**: «Chat Completions (default)» or «Responses API»; the field **Address of this model (optional)**), described by one paragraph of the card (`modelRequestHint`). The page never switches a model by itself: for an address that ends with `/responses` it offers the Responses API with a button (`data-responses-suggestion`), and the notice of a model that refused thinking with tools offers it too (below). The line **Requests go to** says what the engine will send the request to, from the engine's own rule (`modelRequestTarget`: the address built from the integration, `…/openai/responses?api-version=<version>` for Azure OpenAI, `…/openai/v1/responses` for its v1 API, `<base>/responses` for the others; the host and the path, never the query, for an address of its own), and, when the integration keeps a version that predates the Responses API (anything before `2025-03-01`), that the model is asked with `2025-04-01-preview` (`effectiveResponsesApiVersion`; the model's own version wins, whatever it is, and the v1 API has none). GitHub Models has no Responses API: the editor says so, and the engine refuses the request with the same words unless the model has an address of its own. A problem with the address is said in words and with `aria-invalid` (not an address, not http or https, a user name or a password), and a key in its query (`key`, `api-key`, `token`, `sig`, `code`…) is warned of, because the address is saved with the settings and travels to a personal server. A request on the Responses API is the one the Azure portal's playground sends (`input` with a `developer` message, `max_output_tokens`, `reasoning.effort`), with `store: false` (the Responses API keeps what it answers for 30 days unless told not to) and function tools in their flat form (`{ type: "function", name, description, parameters, strict: false }`, forced with `tool_choice: "required"`); its stream (`response.output_text.delta`, `response.function_call_arguments.delta`, `response.completed`, `response.incomplete`, `response.failed`, `error`, and the events that nothing here needs, which are ignored) is read by the code that reads Chat Completions, so the deadlines, the live JSON of a structured reply, the tokens and the failures are the same. The trail says it: `AiRouteStep.protocol` and `AiRouteStep.path` (the path of an address of the model's own; never its query), the fact **Protocol** and the line `Protocol: Responses API` of the diagnostics, the endpoint with its path, the badge of the debug panel (which keeps the protocol and no address) and the records of a call (which keep the protocol and the origin). The personal server approves the address of a model like the base of its integration, and a server that predates engine 0.1.20 does not carry either setting and asks through Chat Completions at the address of the integration, so it needs its own release.

**What the provider said.** A provider that refuses a request is told in its own words, as labelled fields (**HTTP status**, **Message**, **Type**, **Param**, **Code**) and whole (`AiErrorBody`, engine 0.1.19: the engine reads it once, where an HTTP failure becomes an error, whatever its shape: OpenAI, Azure, Anthropic, Gemini, `{ message }`, `{ detail }`, `{ error: "text" }`; a text, the page of a gateway or a JSON nobody knows is shown as it is, cut at about two kilobytes with an ellipsis; a response with no body says `HTTP 400, no body`; every key, bearer token and signature is taken out, and the request and its `Authorization` are never read nor kept). It is in `errorDetail.body` and in each step of the route (`route[].body`: the request that was refused and the one asked again are two steps), so the panel of the autopilot (**What the provider said**, under the facts, in the language of the app, with the HTTP status among them; with several candidates each step of the trail says its own), **Copy diagnostics** (`Provider message:`, `Provider type:`, `Provider param:`, `Provider code:`, in English, with the route `/v1/chat/completions` kept as it is and a path of a disk still taken out), the failed attempts of the records of the calls (`error.body`), the Copilot (the same fields, as the lines of its message, under the name of each candidate that failed) and the debug panel say it. The debug panel keeps the status, the type, the param and the code as short words (an identifier, never a sentence) and not the message, which stays redacted like every error of that history because it is saved in the browser. A failure of an image, speech or transcription request says the same short words from the OpenAI library's error.

**Thinking when tools are used.** Some models refuse a thinking level only when the request carries function tools («Function tools with reasoning_effort are not supported for gpt-6-luna in /v1/chat/completions. To use function tools, use /v1/responses or set reasoning_effort to 'none'.», a 400 whose `param` is `reasoning_effort`). What a model takes with tools depends on its family, and the Azure OpenAI documentation (*reasoning models*, «Tool calling with reasoning models») says so: `gpt-5.6-*` take them with the level left out (and still reason) or with `none`; `gpt-6-sol`, `gpt-6-luna` and `gpt-6.1-terra` refuse a request that leaves the level out as well and take `none` only; `gpt-6-astra`, `gpt-6.1-sol`, `gpt-6.2-astra` and `gpt-6.2-sol` take function tools on the **Responses API only** and refuse `none`. The engine recognises the refusal from the words and the parameter, never from the name of the model (`refusesThinkingWithTools`), and finds out which family by asking: the call is asked again without the level, then with `none`, each way once (a new attempt of the same candidate, so the trail shows every request), and the model is remembered by the way that answered. **Do not use thinking when tools are used** (`ChatModel.noThinkingWithTools`: `true` leaves the level out, `"none"` sends `none`; the checkbox of the model's row, also in a row that has just been added, in English and Italian, ticks it for either) is the same fact set by the author; a request with tools to such a model goes that way from the start, and a request without tools keeps its level. Any other 400 fails at once with its words. A model that no way answers (the `gpt-6.2-sol` family) fails with the provider's last words and what was tried («Narrarium asked again without a thinking level and with "none", and the model still refuses function tools on this endpoint: the provider says to use /v1/responses for them»), and nothing is learned: that model needs the Responses API, and the same words add what to set («Set the "API protocol" of the model to "Responses API" in AI Integrations to use them»). The session keeps what it learned in its memory (`AiModelMemory`), so a run on the personal server learns it at once; the page that hears the `model-learned` event, or the call of the Copilot that learned it, sets the checkbox in the settings of this browser, saves them, and shows a notice (`LearnedModelNotices` under the band of the autopilot; a message that stays for 30 seconds for a call outside the autopilot): «gpt-6-luna: thinking is turned off when tools are used; the provider said so», the provider's words as fields, and the two ways out in plain words, the **Responses API first** (a button, **Use the Responses API for «gpt-6-luna»**, offered for an Azure OpenAI or an OpenAI-compatible integration whose model is in the settings, whatever the provider's words say; `chooseResponsesApi` sets `apiProtocol: "responses"` on that model, saves the settings like any change of them, and the card then says that the model is asked through the Responses API from its next request, with its requests with tools keeping their thinking level; GitHub Models has no Responses API, so the notice offers it nothing), then turning thinking off, which is what was done (and, for a model that takes `none`, that it is asked with «none»). The setting of «Do not use thinking when tools are used» is a rule of Chat Completions: a model asked through the Responses API is sent its level with tools, and the row says the setting is kept but unused. The line of the step that runs says **Thinking: not sent (tools)** for the level left out and **Thinking: off (tools)** for `none` (`thinkingWhy: "tools"` of `call-attempt`, and the same words in the debug panel). The setting is in the configuration that is sent to the personal server (`pickAiRuntimeConfig`): a server that predates engine 0.1.19 does not keep it and does not learn it, so it needs its own release.

## Slow models

Each AI integration has three settings for a slow model, read by the engine in the browser and on the personal server alike (`pickAiRuntimeConfig`). **Provider timeout** (`requestTimeoutMs`) is how long the provider may take to start answering: 10 minutes when empty, never under 30 seconds (a lower value is raised when the call is made, and the setting keeps what was written). **Idle timeout** (`idleTimeoutMs`) is the longest silence between two pieces of a streamed reply: 10 minutes when empty, kept between 30 seconds and an hour in the page, which clamps what is typed. It is the only limit of a silence only when no gateway in between has a shorter one: LlmProxy resets a silent connection after its own `Gateway:UpstreamIdleTimeoutSeconds` (5 minutes unless changed) and Cloudflare closes one after 100 seconds, and a gateway that sends its headers at once (LlmProxy does) makes the wait for the first token a silence of the stream, so the idle timeout, not the provider timeout, is what limits a long prefill. A reply that stops halfway (the connection lost, the stream closed early, an error or an abort reported inside it) is repeated like any passing failure, never applied, and the error panel's technical details say what arrived (`errorDetail.transport`: the HTTP status, bytes and chunks, when the last chunk came, how the stream ended, the tokens, the request id and the end of what arrived). **Streaming** (`streamMode`) is Automatic by default, which streams every request, the structured ones of the autopilot and of the Copilot included; **Off** is only for a provider that refuses streamed replies together with function calls (an HTTP 400 that mentions stream, whose message tells the author to set "Streaming" of the integration to off), and then the provider timeout counts the whole reply. Automatic is stored as no value, so a settings file of an older app is unchanged, and the three fields go wherever the integration goes: the cloud settings, the offline cache, the account replicas and the AI configuration sent to the server. A server older than 0.1.7 ignores the last two. The Copilot (its text, its forced calls and its confirmations, `assistant/copilotAi.ts`) uses the same client through `createBrowserAi`, so it has the same three deadlines, the same streaming and the same sentences when one runs out, and what its author stops with Stop wins over all of them; `routingExecution.maxTotalDurationMs` is counted as the engine counts it (it never interrupts the first candidate, and a fallback starts only while time remains, for the time that is left). Voice and images do not use the engine's client: they count `requestTimeoutMs` as the time of the whole request (60 seconds when empty), and the hint under the field says so.

The Copilot also rides over a passing error, as the SDK it used to call did, but under control (`assistant/quietRetry.ts`, `runCopilotAttempt`). `quietRetryWait` says whether an error of the engine's client is one: a 408 or 429, any 5xx but the 524 of a gateway that ran out of patience (a 500 is not `transient` for the engine, which makes the run stop on an identical one, but it is what a flaky node gives), or a connection that could not be made or was lost; never an error that ran out of time (`timedOut`), a reply that was refused or cut, a prompt that does not fit, the routing limits, the privacy policy, «Tools only», or a 4xx other than 408 and 429. The same candidate is then asked again, at most two more times, after about 1 s and 3 s (±25%, never sooner than the `retryAfterMs` of the error, and none at all when the provider asks for more than 10 s), only while `maxTotalDurationMs` leaves room for the wait and the attempt after it, and the author's Stop (or a change of account) ends the wait at once. A reply that had begun to stream starts over: the text so far is replaced, never added to. Each attempt is an entry of the debug panel and a record of the costs, and the chat is told once for each retry (`assistant/retryNotice.ts`: the notices go to whoever listens to the signal of the request, and `retryNoticeText` makes the sentence, rounded to the second, never a countdown). After the last attempt the error goes on as it did: to the next candidate, or to the author. See *Timeouts and streaming* in the [engine README](../packages/engine/README.md).

## The records of the AI calls

When the book keeps records (the host passes `calls`, see *Configuration* in the [`narrarium-engine` README](../packages/engine/README.md#ai-calls)), every AI call of a task is recorded as it runs: the system and the user message as they were sent, the reply as it streams, and each provider attempt, with the candidates of the route that the call left out beside them (engine 0.1.17: an attempt with `outcome: "skipped"` and `skip`, the reason and its figures; no duration, no usage, no error). The records are kept by the host (the browser's database, or the files of the personal server); the engine sends them nowhere.

- **Recorded:** the system and user messages (each one limited to 1 MiB, its head kept), the reply (its text, or the JSON of a structured reply, as it arrives; a reply longer than 2 MiB keeps its head and its last 256 KiB), the status of the call and of each attempt, the endpoint *origin* of each attempt (`https://api.openai.com`, never its path or its query), the model, the usage, the cost, the transport facts the client keeps (status, bytes, how the stream ended), and a cleaned error. The record of a task (its title, kind, chapter, scene, status and times) outlives the last 200 entries of `autopilot/activity.json`.
- **Never recorded:** keys, headers (`Authorization`, `api-key`, cookies), full addresses, tokens, and the words of the reasoning (only their count). Every string goes through the redaction of the engine and through the secrets the host gives it before it is written.
- **Written as it streams:** the text is written every 2 seconds or every 16 KiB, and at the end. A call that the run stops in the middle of its reply ends as `aborted`, with the text that arrived, marked `partial`. A call whose stream is cut ends as `interrupted`; the retry of its step is a new activity with its own calls.
- **Budget:** 256 MiB per book in a browser, 1 GiB per device, 2 GiB per book on a personal server, and no write on a server with less than 1 GiB free. After each task the oldest finished tasks that are not pinned are dropped, whole, to keep the book within its budget. A task the author pins (`keep`) is never dropped.
- **When the records fail:** a store that fails (or is full) never fails a step and never slows it. The run says it once, with the insight `callsStoreFailed`, and goes on without records until the next run.

The events of the calls carry their ids, sizes and times, never their text: `call-started` (the call, its label, whether it is structured, the size of its system and user messages) and `call-ended` (its status, its attempts, the characters and bytes kept, whether it is partial, how long it took, and whether a limit dropped part of it). A `task-progress` event carries the `callId` of its call when the book keeps records, and always `callSeq`, the call's number in its step; every host follows the events with the engine's `applyLiveProgress`, so what is shown of a call never flickers between its text and «answering», never goes back to nothing within the call, and is never the text of another call.

A structured step streams its JSON too: while the model writes the arguments of its function, the progress of the step carries their tail (the last 700 characters, `kind: "json"`), so that the page shows the reply as it is written. Only the providers whose stream the client reads send it (the OpenAI-compatible ones); the others say only the numbers they always did.

## What the debug panel keeps of an attempt

The panel of AI requests keeps metadata only, never a prompt or a reply. For each chat attempt of the autopilot and of the Copilot it adds what the engine says through `onAttempt` and `onAttemptEnd`: whether a structured answer was asked for as a function call or as JSON in the message, whether the request streams (and whether the reply was read as a stream), the level of thinking that was sent (and, when it went as the argument of the chat template, that), whether the function was called, the models «Tools only» left out, the time to the first byte, the state of «Tools only» and the deadlines the attempt ran with, and which of them ran out. Every field is optional, so an entry saved by an older version shows none of them; the store keeps only flags, numbers and the few words the engine uses, whatever it is given, and the history keeps its limit of size. `debug/attemptBadges.ts` is pure and turns the fields into the badges of the panel.

## Where the AI calls are kept: the copies

The records of the autopilot's AI calls (what it sent, what came back, each attempt; see *AI calls* in the [engine README](../packages/engine/README.md)) are always kept on the device that ran them (the browser's IndexedDB, and the personal server's private folder when the book runs there). The settings of the autopilot have a tab, **AI calls** (`#autopilot-calls`), where the author can also copy every *finished* task to **one** more place, chosen per book and saved with the account's settings (`aiCallsCopy` of the book: `github`, `google-drive` or `onedrive`; absent means nowhere else). The choice is not a run setting: it can change at any time, also while the autopilot runs, and it saves on change.

**Who copies.** The browser, from its own local store (`src/autopilot/calls/copies/`: `CallsCopier` reads finished tasks through the engine's `AiCallStore` port), so a book that runs on a personal server is copied too, once the browser has brought its records local, and the server needs no Google, Microsoft or GitHub credentials. A copy never stops a run and never blocks the page: what is waiting is in a persistent outbox (`CopyOutbox`: the tasks still to copy, with their attempts, the next time to try and the last failure) and is retried with a growing wait (30 seconds, doubling up to an hour), when the device is back online, when a token is renewed, and every five minutes; it resumes after a reload. An account change drops the outbox (each item carries the account scope it was queued under, the same fence the repository operations have). A task already copied is never copied twice: the file name is made from the task alone, and what was copied is in a ledger (`CopyLedger`).

**The file.** One file per task, `YYYY/MM/DD/<HHmmss>-<activityId>.ndjson.gz` (the UTC time the task started), under the root of the target. It is a gzip of newline-delimited JSON, so `gunzip -c <file> | jq .` reads it without Narrarium:

- line 1: `{"type":"task","formatVersion":1,"exportedAt":"<when the task ended>","header":{…}}`, the task header (`bookId`, `activityId`, `title`, `kind`, `chapter`, `scene`, `status`, `startedAt`, `endedAt`, `calls`, `bytes`, `pinned`);
- then one line per call, in the order of `seq`: `{"type":"call","record":{…},"system":"…","user":"…","responses":[{"attempt":1,"text":"…"}]}`. `record` is the call's `AiCallRecord` (identity, request sizes, status, each provider attempt with its endpoint origin, timing, usage, cost and cleaned error), `system` and `user` are the texts as the call kept them, and each entry of `responses` is the text of the provider attempt whose `record.attempts[].textAttempt` is `attempt` (an attempt that returned no text has none).

`formatVersion` is the engine's `AI_CALLS_FORMAT_VERSION`. A reader skips a line whose `type` it does not know and keeps a file of a newer version as it is, saying so; the site exports `decodeTaskFile` (bytes to task) and `parseTaskFileText` as that reader. Keys, headers, URLs and tokens are never in a record: the stores redact before writing.

| Target | Where | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| GitHub | A dedicated **orphan branch**, `narrarium-ai-calls`, of the book's repository (never the working branch), under `calls/`. One commit per batch (at most 40 files and 24 MiB), fast-forward only: history is never rewritten. | **Public repositories are refused**, checked before every batch (GitHub keeps history after a deletion). 512 MiB for the archive, then copying stops with a warning until the author deletes the copies. A file over 50 MiB is skipped. The branch is deleted by «Delete the copies». |
| Google Drive | The hidden **app-data folder** (`drive.appdata`): `ai-calls/<bookId>/YYYY/MM/DD/<file>`, with the connection's token. | 2 GiB per book: the oldest day-folders are deleted first. A file over 100 MiB is skipped. |
| OneDrive | `Apps/Narrarium/ai-calls/<bookId>/YYYY/MM/DD/<file>` (the app folder, `Files.ReadWrite`), after the folder and its ownership marker are made as the account sync makes them. | 2 GiB per book, trimmed like Drive. |

No new OAuth scope is needed: Drive app-data, OneDrive's app folder and the book's GitHub token are what the connections already have. A sign-in that has to be renewed holds the copy as «Reconnect» (the same classes as the account sync: `credential-expired`, MSAL's and Google's renewal codes). The copies in an account stay until the author deletes them there: «Delete the copies in <place>» asks to confirm and is the only thing that removes them; deleting the records of a book deletes the device's and the server's together, and says so.
